# Cultural Christianity Adapter

## Purpose

The Cultural Christianity Adapter supports institutions serving learners who carry a Christian heritage — through family, community, holiday tradition, or cultural identity — without holding active doctrinal belief or formal church membership. This adapter honors formation shaped by Christian culture as a legitimate and distinct starting point.

-----

## Who This Adapter Serves

- individuals who identify as Christian on surveys but do not practice
- people shaped by Christian ethics, aesthetics, and calendar without theological conviction
- lapsed or de-churched individuals carrying residual Christian moral frameworks
- nominal Christians engaging education, governance, or theological content from a heritage position
- researchers, writers, and creatives working within or alongside Christian cultural frameworks
- second and third-generation post-religious individuals still navigating inherited Christian identity

-----

## Optional Components

A Cultural Christianity implementation may include:

- biblical narrative literacy as cultural heritage (not doctrinal commitment)
- the Sermon on the Mount as an embedded moral architecture
- Christian liturgical calendar as seasonal and communal identity rhythm
- Christmas, Easter, and Pentecost as cultural formation anchors
- Christian hymnody, art, and architectural tradition as aesthetic formation
- Christian ethics in Western law, governance, and civic tradition
- family and communal ritual practice as identity formation (baptism, burial, marriage)
- C.S. Lewis, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky, and literary Christianity as intellectual entry points
- the concept of grace, forgiveness, and redemption as secular ethical inheritance
- saints, martyrs, and Christian historical memory as narrative formation

-----

## Formation Outcomes

Learners may demonstrate:

- literacy in Christian cultural narrative, symbol, and calendar
- comprehension of Christian ethics as an embedded cultural formation system
- ability to engage theological texts from a heritage rather than confessional position
- awareness of how Christian formation shapes Western legal, civic, and aesthetic tradition
- integration of Christian cultural identity without requiring doctrinal affirmation
- capacity for interfaith dialogue grounded in inherited Christian cultural fluency
- reflective engagement with the question of what one believes vs. what one has inherited

-----

## Adapter Principle

The Cultural Christianity adapter supports heritage-centered, narrative-fluent, and non-confessional formation while remaining mapped to the shared CBE infrastructure.

-----

## Notes for Implementation

- This adapter requires no doctrinal affirmation from the learner — formation is heritage-based.
- It may serve as a transitional bridge toward the Messianic, Catholic, Orthodox, or Secular adapters depending on the learner’s trajectory.
- The question “what do I actually believe?” is built into this adapter as a legitimate formation inquiry.
- Literary Christianity modules (Lewis, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky) are compatible with Secular and Gnostic adapter content.
