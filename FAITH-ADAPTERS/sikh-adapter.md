# Sikh Faith Adapter

## Purpose

The Sikh Faith Adapter supports institutions integrating the Guru Granth Sahib, Khalsa formation, Sikh ethics, and the Gurmukhi-anchored tradition of Sikhi into educational and governance frameworks.

-----

## Optional Components

A Sikh implementation may include:

- Guru Granth Sahib as eternal living Guru and primary scriptural authority
- Ten Sikh Gurus and Sikh historical formation
- Waheguru — Ik Onkar and the theology of divine unity
- Three Pillars of Sikhi: Naam Japna, Kirat Karni, Vand Chakna
- Khalsa initiation (Amrit Sanchar) and the Five Ks (Panj Kakars)
- Sikh ethics: Seva (selfless service), Simran (meditative remembrance), Sewa
- Nitnem — daily prayer and liturgical formation
- Langar as a model of radical equality and community governance
- Sikh history, Gurbani poetry, and kirtan tradition
- Gurmukhi script literacy (optional)

-----

## Formation Outcomes

Learners may demonstrate:

- foundational literacy in Sikh scripture and the Guru Granth Sahib
- applied understanding of the Three Pillars as an ethical formation framework
- comprehension of Waheguru and Ik Onkar as doctrinal and philosophical anchors
- Khalsa identity and its institutional implications
- civic and governance formation grounded in Seva and radical equality
- integration of Sikh historical formation into institutional identity
- awareness of Gurdwara community structures as governance models

-----

## Adapter Principle

The Sikh adapter supports Gurbani-centered and Khalsa-formation-aligned development while remaining mapped to the shared CBE infrastructure.

-----

## Notes for Implementation

- Gurmukhi literacy components may be integrated at the discretion of the implementing institution.
- Langar and Seva community models may be cross-referenced with DAO and cooperative governance modules.
- Interfaith comparative modules may draw on shared Abrahamic and Dharmic lineage.
